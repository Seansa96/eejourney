# Three Phase Systems

The issue with Single Phase is that it cannot produce a steady voltage, as you can see it oscillates between positive and negative.  That means there are times, though near undetectable to the human eye, that the current does not flow in the same direction.

This can be rectified with the use of two additional coils, for three in total, typically positioned 120 degrees apart. It is possible to use more, but this is the intersection of simple and effective (and cheap).

Why 120 degrees apart? Well, there are 360 degrees in a circle, so $360 / 3 = 120$.

![[Pasted image 20260124164325.png]]

Typical nomenclature refers to these as red, yellow, and blue phases.


# Three Phase Voltage

As the waves are 120 degrees apart, we can show this on a phasor diagram quite easily.

![[Pasted image 20260124164715.png]]

The phase to phase voltage can be seen by simply observing the vector between two phase voltages.

![[Pasted image 20260124164945.png]]

Notice that this line is far longer than the other lines, so the phase to phase (also called line) voltage is greater than the phase to neutral voltage, which is just the distance of each individual phase vector from the middle of the phasor diagram.

Typically, this line voltage is $1.73$ times longer than the phase to neutral voltage.

# Three Phase Equations

## Voltage
$$ Line Voltage (V_L) = 1.73 * Phase Voltage (V_{PH})$$

Then, there are **3** phase voltages, and **3** line voltages.


| Phase V        | Line V      |
| -------------- | ----------- |
| Red-Neutral    | Red-Yellow  |
| Yellow-Neutral | Red-Blue    |
| Blue-Neutral   | Yellow-Blue |

## Current

The line current is similarly typically $1.73$ times greater than the phase current, due to scaling off of voltage.

$$
Line Current (I_L) = 1.73 * Phase Current(I_{PH})
$$


| Phase I | Line I      |
| ------- | ----------- |
| Red     | Red-Yellow  |
| Yellow  | Red-Blue    |
| Blue    | Yellow-Blue |

## Power

$$
P_{3PH} = V_L * I_L * PF (cos\theta)
$$
**$PF$ is the power factor.

It is sometimes difficult to measure the line current, so we may see a different version of this equation, using the **phase current.**

$$
P_{3PH} = 1.73 * V_L * I_{PH} * PF(cos\theta)
$$

# Frequency

The frequency of an AC system is the number of sine wave cycles that occur every minute.  *Why is it the number of sine wave cycles?*

Answer:
	Because each full cycle of a sine wave represents the voltage going from positive to negative one time.

The faster the magnet spins the greater the frequency. Recall that the power of the magnet is derived from the current, so the higher the current, the greater the frequency.

Frequency is measured in Hertz, in honor of Heinrich Hertz, who proved the presence of Electromagnetic Waves.

**1 Hertz = 1 Sine Wave (or 1 cycle) per second**

In the **UK**, AC systems are scheduled at a frequency of 50 $Hz$ while the US has a frequency of 60 $Hz$ for our power systems. 

Note that two AC systems of different frequencies cannot be combined without some sort of rectification of their waveforms to match.
