
# History of Electricity

In 1831, [[Michael Faraday]] made the discovery of Electromagnetic Induction.  He did this by attaching two wires to a disc and rotated it between the two poles of a horseshoe magnet, and by doing so created an electric current through induction.

# Quick Terms

* Voltage - A measure of electric potential energy difference.
* Current - The measure of how much electrical charge flows 
* Resistance - The amount of force a material uses to oppose current.
* Conductors - Substances such as copper or aluminum that have little resistance.
* Insulators - Substances such as wood, rubber, or glass that have a high resistance to electricity.

# Direct Current

![[Pasted image 20260123235044.png]]
The main source of DC power is from batteries.  Current from batteries flows from the positive terminal through the load and back to the negative terminal.  

In a DC system, the current only flows in one direction.  It is used in most electronic devices as well as cars.  

It is relatively stable and does not change its form over time.  It can also be stored in chemical form whereas AC cannot.

However, for exactly the same reason it is ideal for storage and most electronic devices, this makes it difficult when we are dealing with a large amount of electrical energy. This is why AC is used for most electrical supply networks. This is why power lines deal with AC and use transformers to step the voltage down to DC.

# Alternating Current

Main source of AC power will be generally from electrical networks or generators. The symbol is usually a circle with a sine wave inside, like this.

![[Pasted image 20260123235134.png]]

AC power flows from **live** to the load to **neutral**, as opposed to **positive** to load to **negative** in the DC systems.

Alternating current, as its name suggests, follows the shape of a sine wave and oscillates between positive and negative over time.

Once AC has been generated, it must be used straight away.  However, it is fairly easy to transform and manipulate.

# Back to Voltage

Note that the ground usually has 0 volts.  When clouds in the sky rub together when conditions are right, they generate a voltage potential due to static electricity.

We can classify the clouds as the live element and the air as the conductor. Due to the eventual enormous difference in voltage potential between the ground, which has 0 volts, and the clouds, a discharge occurs that overcomes the heavy resistance of air, turning it into a conductor and allowing current to flow.  

Flow of current continues as long as electric potential difference exists.  

To use electricity, we need 4 elements:

* A voltage source - to provide an electric power difference
* A live or positive conductor - to take the energy to the load
* A load - to use the electricity
* A neutral or negative conductor - to take the energy from the load to the voltage source
