# Magnetic Fields

When we pass DC through a conductor, a magnetic field is produced perpendicular to the current flow. The greater the current, the stronger the magnetic field.  *Does the magnetic field constantly move at all, or is it stable because of the DC?*

If we introduce another magnetic field near the conductor, it will cause the conductor to move. The direction of this motion can be ascertained by **Fleming's Left Hand Rule**.

We attach the rotor to a DC supply.  When current is applied, we refer to the left hand rule, which tells us that on the left conductor, the rotor will be forced downwards, whereas the right conductor will force it upwards as the current moves downward. This will be 180 degrees counter-clockwise. *Why is it downwards on the left and upwards on the right? How do we move the pieces of the left hand rule?*

To keep the rotor spinning in the same direction, the current must flow in the same direction. This is done by adding a commutator, which ensures that the flow of current on each side of the rotor stays the same as it rotates. *What exactly is a commutator?*
