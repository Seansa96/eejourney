
French scientist [[Charles Augustin de Coulumb]] is the one who created and formalized **Coulumb's Law**, otherwise known as the **Inverse Square Law**.  This states that the force of electrostatic attraction or repulsion is directly proportional to the product of the two charges, and inversely proportional to the square of the distance between them.

A Coulumb, as in the unit, is defined as the charge transferred by 1 ampere of current in one second.

# 2.2 The Ampere

Named for [[Andre Ampere]], a scientist famous for his work dealing with electromagnetism.  The ampere $A$ is equal to one coulumb per second.

# 2.3 The Electron Flow Theory

This states that because electrons are negative particles, current flows from the most negative point in the circuit to the most positive. This is the most widely accepted theory.

# 2.4 The Conventional Current Flow Theory

This is the second of the two main theories concerning current flow, which was defined by Benjamin Franklin.  This states that current flows from the most positive point to the most negative. Even though most of the world uses the Electron Flow Theory as it has almost been proved within certainty, it is sometimes useful to envision the application of this theory in electric circuits.

Most electronic circuits use the negative terminal as the ground or common reference point. When the negative terminal is used as the ground, the positive terminal is said to be above ground, or "hot". It is more intuitive for people to think of something flowing down rather than up, or from a point above ground. In an automobile, the positive terminal is typically considering the live or hot terminal.  

Because of this, many schematics and drawings assume the conventional current flow theory.
