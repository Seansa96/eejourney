A conductor is an element that facilitates free mobility of electrons between atoms. The property that this is measured by is resistivity, which allows them to easily pass an electrical current due to the amount of free electrons floating around their atomic structure.

When a positive voltage is applied, (creating a voltage difference, or a voltage gradient), this incites electron drift from the parent atom through a media or medium.  This is known as [[electrical current|current]]. 

Examples of good conductors are [[Aluminum]], [[Copper]], [[Silver]], and [[Gold]]. These are all metals, which generally have good conductivity, however, elements such as [[Carbon]], which does not manifest in nature as such as is a non-metal, are also good conductors. This is because these elements also, as mentioned before, have free electrons orbiting their atomic nucleus, which we refer to as [[Valence electrons]].

It is important to note that a conductor still has some measure of resistance--it is just that it is low comparatively to other connecting or surrounding atoms of differing elements.

Note also that the energy lost in the travel of electrons through such materials manifests as heat, which makes it important for conductors to be able to dissipate this energy well when under moderate to heavy load for long term use.
