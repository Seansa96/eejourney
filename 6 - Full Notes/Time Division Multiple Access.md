# Time Division Multiple Access

In the swap from 1G to 2G, the [[GSM]] found that better bandwidth optimization schemes were in need as the allotment of frequency bands had not yet been changed. So, by way of enhanced digital signal compression, the audio transmission of a single user could be reduced so that a single channel could contain multiple users at one time, and by allocating each user a time slot, that channel could accommodate simultaneous transmission

This is accomplished primarily through the Pulse Repetition Frequency mechanism (PRF), 
which uses a frame as an encapsulation for the timeslots, that being typically around 8. Each timeslot is defined as being around .577 ms long, for a total of frame duration of 4.615 ms.  Thus, every device may transmit their data within these defined frame lengths, with the frequency being around 217 Hz, or 217 frames a second.  This far enhanced the efficiency of communications, which previously allotted an entire channel to a user for the duration of their call, whereas now a time-sharing system was instantiated.
