# Mathematics CIR — Remediation Protocols

---

# Score Band: 1–6

# Fragmented Understanding

## Characteristics

- Cannot reliably explain the concept
    
- Procedural steps feel arbitrary
    
- Equations appear disconnected from meaning
    
- Heavy dependence on examples or memorization
    
- Fails to identify when concepts apply
    

## Primary Goal

Build conceptual anchors and symbolic familiarity.

---

## Core Remediation Protocol

### 1. Identity Reconstruction

Ask:

- What is this concept fundamentally describing?
    
- What changes, relationships, or structures does it model?
    

Avoid formulas initially.

---

### 2. Visual Grounding

- Graph the behavior if possible
    
- Draw geometric interpretations
    
- Use diagrams whenever applicable
    
- Connect symbols to physical or geometric meaning
    

---

### 3. Equation Interpretation

For every equation:

- What does each variable represent?
    
- What changes?
    
- What remains invariant?
    
- Why does the equation structurally make sense?
    

Do NOT memorize formulas in isolation.

---

### 4. Guided Repetition

Perform:

- low-difficulty,
    
- high-volume,
    
- highly similar problems.
    

Goal:  
Build symbolic familiarity without overload.

---

## Recommended Activities

- Derive formulas from simple cases
    
- Explain concepts aloud
    
- Rewrite textbook examples step-by-step
    
- Create variable-definition tables
    
- Perform substitution drills
    
- Use graphing tools heavily
    

---

## Common Friction Patterns

|Friction|Repair|
|---|---|
|Symbolic|Algebra drills|
|Visual|Graphing/geometric interpretation|
|Translation|Rewrite word problems symbolically|
|Memory|Derive instead of memorize|
|Abstraction|Use concrete examples first|

---

# Score Band: 7-10

# Localized Competency

## Characteristics

- Can solve familiar problems
    
- Understands portions of the structure
    
- Difficulty transferring knowledge to new forms
    
- Recognizes concepts after prompting
    
- Weak connections between related ideas
    

## Primary Goal

Build structural linkage and pattern recognition.

---

## Core Remediation Protocol

### 1. Variation Exposure

Solve:

- multiple forms,
    
- representations,
    
- and orientations of the same problem type.
    

Goal:  
Separate the underlying structure from surface appearance.

---

### 2. System Mapping

Ask:

- What topics connect to this?
    
- What assumptions does this depend on?
    
- What later topics depend on this?
    

Build “subject tree” awareness.

---

### 3. Translation Practice

Focus specifically on:

- converting scenarios into equations,
    
- identifying known/unknown variables,
    
- selecting governing relationships.
    

---

### 4. Error Taxonomy

Track recurring mistakes:

- sign errors,
    
- missing conditions,
    
- theorem misuse,
    
- algebra collapse,
    
- premature simplification.
    

Categorize failures instead of merely correcting them.

---

## Recommended Activities

- Mixed-problem sets
    
- “Identify the method” drills
    
- Reverse engineering solved problems
    
- Derivation reconstruction
    
- Teaching concepts from memory
    
- Timed symbolic manipulation
    

---

## Common Friction Patterns

|Friction|Repair|
|---|---|
|Pattern Recognition|Mixed exposure|
|Translation|Word-to-symbol exercises|
|Procedural|Timed repetition|
|Structural Isolation|Concept mapping|
|Retrieval Latency|Rapid recall drills|

---

# Score Band: 11–14

# Integrated Functional Understanding

## Characteristics

- Reliable problem-solving ability
    
- Understands concepts from first principles
    
- Can explain relationships between ideas
    
- Still struggles with:
    
    - edge cases,
        
    - novel synthesis,
        
    - speed,
        
    - or abstraction depth
        

## Primary Goal

Increase flexibility, compression, and transferability.

---

## Core Remediation Protocol

### 1. Novel Problem Exposure

Seek:

- unfamiliar problems,
    
- olympiad-style twists,
    
- conceptual inversions,
    
- proof-oriented questions.
    

Goal:  
Stress-test schema flexibility.

---

### 2. Reconstruction Practice

Attempt to:

- derive formulas,
    
- rebuild methods,
    
- and justify assumptions  
    without notes.
    

---

### 3. Compression Training

Practice reducing concepts into:

- core invariants,
    
- governing principles,
    
- or generalized operators.
    

Goal:  
Move from procedural knowledge to structural fluency.

---

### 4. Latency Reduction

Focus on:

- recognition speed,
    
- symbolic fluency,
    
- and mental chunking.
    

---

## Recommended Activities

- Timed mixed sets
    
- First-principles derivations
    
- Proof sketches
    
- Teaching others
    
- Explaining why methods fail
    
- Edge-case analysis
    

---

## Common Friction Patterns

|Friction|Repair|
|---|---|
|Latency|Timed retrieval|
|Edge Cases|Boundary-condition drills|
|Transfer Weakness|Novel problem exposure|
|Over-Procedural Thinking|First-principles reconstruction|
|Abstraction Gaps|Generalization exercises|

---

# Score Band: 15–16

# Generalized Transfer Mastery

## Characteristics

- Strong conceptual integration
    
- High procedural fluency
    
- Handles unfamiliar problems effectively
    
- Can connect topics across domains
    
- Understands both operationally and structurally
    

## Primary Goal

Refinement, generalization, and creative synthesis.

---

## Core Remediation Protocol

### 1. Cross-Domain Integration

Connect mathematics to:

- physics,
    
- programming,
    
- signal processing,
    
- circuits,
    
- optimization,
    
- statistics,
    
- geometry,
    
- or abstract systems.
    

---

### 2. Theory Deepening

Explore:

- proofs,
    
- alternative formulations,
    
- historical derivations,
    
- generalized cases,
    
- and formal abstraction.
    

---

### 3. Creative Construction

Create:

- original problems,
    
- alternate solution methods,
    
- visual explanations,
    
- or conceptual simplifications.
    

---

### 4. Weak-Link Elimination

Identify:

- rare failure modes,
    
- computation bottlenecks,
    
- or narrow conceptual blind spots.
    

---

## Recommended Activities

- Olympiad problems
    
- Proof-heavy work
    
- Advanced applications
    
- Teaching and tutoring
    
- System synthesis
    
- Research-style exploration
    

---

## Common Friction Patterns

| Friction                  | Repair                         |
| ------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Narrow Blind Spots        | Targeted edge-case work        |
| Computational Bottlenecks | Automation fluency             |
| Over-Abstraction          | Concrete application           |
| Theory Gaps               | Formal proof study             |
| Speed                     | Competitive/timed environments |
