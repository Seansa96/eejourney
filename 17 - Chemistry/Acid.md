An acid is formally defined, under [[Bronsted-Lowry]] and [[Arrhenius]], as a compound with the ability to donate protons, which classically requires the presence of a [[Hydrogen]] ion as the donator.  

Thus, an acid *must* contain a Hydrogen ion to qualify as an acid.  Due to their ability to weaken the electron configuration of the atomic bonds that comprise most substances, matter, or materials, strong acids serve as strong corrosives.

An acid also possesses properties such as having a sour taste, dissolving in water, turning [[litmus paper]] red, and being able to react with [[Base|Bases]] to form salts.
