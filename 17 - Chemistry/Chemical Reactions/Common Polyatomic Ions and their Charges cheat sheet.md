## Oxyanion naming patterns

For ions with the same central element:

| Prefix / ending | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `hypo- ... -ite` | Fewest oxygen atoms |
| `... -ite` | Fewer oxygen atoms |
| `... -ate` | More oxygen atoms |
| `per- ... -ate` | Most oxygen atoms |

### Chlorine example

| Name | Formula |
|---|---|
| Hypochlorite | `ClO-` |
| Chlorite | `ClO2-` |
| Chlorate | `ClO3-` |
| Perchlorate | `ClO4-` |

---

## `-ite` vs. `-ate`

For the same central atom:

- `-ite` has **one fewer oxygen** than `-ate`.
- The charge usually stays the same.

Examples:

| `-ite` ion | `-ate` ion |
|---|---|
| Nitrite: `NO2-` | Nitrate: `NO3-` |
| Sulfite: `SO3^2-` | Sulfate: `SO4^2-` |
| Chlorite: `ClO2-` | Chlorate: `ClO3-` |

---

## Hydrogen-containing ions

Adding hydrogen reduces the negative charge by `1` each time.

| Ion | Formula | Charge |
|---|---|---:|
| Carbonate | `CO3^2-` | `-2` |
| Hydrogen carbonate | `HCO3-` | `-1` |
| Phosphate | `PO4^3-` | `-3` |
| Hydrogen phosphate | `HPO4^2-` | `-2` |
| Dihydrogen phosphate | `H2PO4-` | `-1` |

> Hydrogen carbonate is also called bicarbonate.  
> Hydrogen sulfate is also called bisulfate.

---

## Formula-writing reminder

Use parentheses when you need more than one polyatomic ion.

Examples:

- Calcium nitrate: `Ca(NO3)2`
- Aluminum sulfate: `Al2(SO4)3`
- Ammonium phosphate: `(NH4)3PO4`
- Magnesium hydroxide: `Mg(OH)2`

Do **not** use parentheses if there is only one polyatomic ion:

- Sodium nitrate: `NaNO3`
- Calcium carbonate: `CaCO3`^
