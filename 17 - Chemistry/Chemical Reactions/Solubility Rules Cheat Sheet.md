# Precipitate Reactions — Quick Cheat Sheet

## How to tell whether a precipitate forms

For two aqueous ionic compounds:

1. Identify the positive ion and negative ion in each reactant.
2. Swap partners to predict the products.
3. Use the solubility rules below on the **products**.
4. If either product is insoluble, it forms a solid precipitate, written `(s)`.
5. If both products are soluble, write **no reaction**.

> A double-displacement reaction is only a possible ion swap.
> A precipitate forms only if the swap creates an insoluble ionic compound.

---

## Always soluble

- Compounds containing a **Group 1 ion**  
  Examples: `Li+`, `Na+`, `K+`

- Compounds containing **ammonium**  
  `NH4+`

- **Nitrates**  
  `NO3-`

- Usually, **acetates**  
  `C2H3O2-` or `CH3COO-`

---

## Usually soluble — know the exceptions

- **Chlorides, bromides, iodides**  
  `Cl-`, `Br-`, `I-`  
  Insoluble with: `Ag+`, `Pb2+`, `Hg2^2+`

- **Sulfates**  
  `SO4^2-`  
  Insoluble or only slightly soluble with: `Ba2+`, `Sr2+`, `Pb2+`  
  `Ca2+` is also commonly treated as an exception.

---

## Usually insoluble — know the exceptions

- **Carbonates**  
  `CO3^2-`  
  Exception: soluble with Group 1 ions and `NH4+`

- **Phosphates**  
  `PO4^3-`  
  Exception: soluble with Group 1 ions and `NH4+`

- **Hydroxides**  
  `OH-`  
  Exception: soluble with Group 1 ions and `NH4+`  
  `Ca2+`, `Sr2+`, and `Ba2+` hydroxides are more soluble.

- **Sulfides**  
  `S^2-`  
  Exception: soluble with Group 1 ions, Group 2 ions, and `NH4+`

---

## Fast memory rule

**Always soluble:**

- Group 1
- Ammonium
- Nitrates

**Usually insoluble:**

- Carbonates
- Phosphates
- Hydroxides
- Sulfides

---

## Reaction-check template

`AB(aq) + CD(aq) → AD + CB`

1. Swap the ions.
2. Check `AD` and `CB`.
3. If one is insoluble:

`AB(aq) + CD(aq) → AD(s) + CB(aq)`

4. If both remain soluble:

**No reaction**
