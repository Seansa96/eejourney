What is matter? It can be defined as anything that takes up space and has mass. *So does that mean if it takes up space, but has no mass, and vice versa, then it is not matter?*

Solids, Liquids, and Gasses are the three states of matter. There is a fourth state that is more rare and does not occur often in nature called plasma, which is a gaseous state containing a large amount of charged particles.

Some samples of matter can behave like other states, like sand behaving similar to a liquid, in that you can pour it into a container and it will take the shape of it, and have a fixed volume.

The mass of an object is the measure of the amount of matter in it. A way to measure the mass of an object is to determine how much force it takes to accelerate it, since $F = M * A$. More commonly however, we use a reference mass and compare an object's mass on a scale to it.

This is different measurement of course than weight, which is the measure of the force exerted by gravity upon an object.  This force is actually directly proportional to the weight of the object.  Therefore, the weight of an object changes with local gravity, but its mass does not.

## Classifications of Matter

Matter can be categorized into several categories, but two main ones can be used to broadly describe them.  Pure substances are those with a constant composition.  *What does that mean exactly?*

All specimens of a pure substance have exactly the same makeup and properties.  Things like melting point, boiling point, color, and sweetness.

**Example:** Sucrose (table sugar) has a composition of 42.1% carbon, 6.5% hydrogen, and 51.4% oxygen.  If it doesn't have this makeup, it is not Sucrose.

Pure substances can be further divided into elements and compounds. Elements are substances which cannot be further broken down through chemical change. Compounds are those that *can* be further broken down into elements or other compounds. Combined elements often have different properties than their individual states.

**Example:** Mercury(II) Oxide can be broken down into Mercury and Oxygen through heating it. 

**Most substances actually break down through heating**.

Distillation is another process, which relies on the volatility of the components, which is how readily a substance may become a gas. In most simple distillation, the mixture is heated at the lowest temperature that will cause the most volatile component to evaporate, whereupon it passes through a cooled tube, a condenser, into a flask or other container that contains the final distillate.

Chromatography is another method which uses a system with two phases of matter, and exploits the affinity of the components in the mixture for the different phases. There is typically a stationary, solid phase, and a mobile, liquid or gas phase.

An example of such is paper chromatography, where a piece of paper is used as the stationary phase. A drop of the mixture to be separated is placed on the paper, and then it is dipped into a liquid that travels up the paper. The different components within the solution travel to different parts of the paper, separating them into a spectrum.

### Mixtures

A mixture is a combination of two or more elements that can be separated by physical changes, like evaporation.  Mixtures that vary in composition are called heterogeneous.  Mixtures that maintain uniformity throughout are homogeneous. 

### Atom

Atoms are the smallest unit of an element that can not be further divided, can enter into a chemical combination, and that has the properties of that element.  Its name actually comes from the greek word, atomos--which means, indivisible. Lastly, a molecule is simply the joining of two or more atoms by chemical bonds.
