### Narration

Hi, I’m Sean Sattaur, and today I’ll show you how chemistry surrounds us through something most of us use every day: computers and mobile phones.

We often think of these devices as products of computer science, electrical engineering, or physics. However, none of them could exist without chemistry.

## Slide 2 — A Collection of Chemical Elements

### Put on the slide

**Your phone is a miniature collection of elements**

- Silicon
    
- Copper
    
- Gold and silver
    
- Lithium and cobalt
    
- Indium and tin
    
- Rare-earth elements
    

### Narration

A smartphone or computer may look like one solid object, but it contains dozens of different chemical elements.

Silicon is used in computer processors. Copper carries electric current through wires and circuit boards. Small amounts of gold and silver are used in electrical connections because they conduct electricity well.

Lithium, cobalt, nickel, manganese, and carbon may be used in rechargeable batteries. Indium and tin are commonly used in touchscreen materials. Rare-earth elements can be found in speakers, microphones, vibration motors, and other components.

These elements are selected because of their chemical and physical properties. Some conduct electricity, some can store chemical energy, some resist corrosion, and others interact with light or produce strong magnetic fields.

The United States Geological Survey explains that smartphones contain dozens of elements in metals, compounds, and composite materials. In other words, a phone is almost like a small, carefully engineered periodic table.

## Slide 3 — Silicon and Semiconductor Chips

### Put on the slide

**Silicon: from sand to processor**

- Silicon is a semiconductor
    
- Four valence electrons
    
- Conductivity can be controlled
    
- “Doping” adds other elements
    
- Transistors process information
    

### Narration

One of the most important elements in modern technology is silicon.

Silicon is obtained from minerals such as quartz, which is primarily silicon dioxide, with the chemical formula SiO₂. Manufacturers chemically process and purify this material until they obtain extremely pure silicon.

Silicon is useful because it is a semiconductor. A semiconductor conducts electricity better than an insulator, such as glass, but not as freely as a conductor, such as copper.

A silicon atom has four valence electrons. In a crystal of pure silicon, each silicon atom forms covalent bonds with nearby silicon atoms. These bonds create an organized structure, but pure silicon does not conduct enough electricity for most electronic applications.

Chemists and engineers modify its conductivity through a process called doping. Doping means adding a very small amount of another element to the silicon crystal.

For example, phosphorus has five valence electrons. When phosphorus is added to silicon, it provides an extra electron that can move through the material. Boron has three valence electrons, so adding boron creates an electron deficiency called a hole.

By placing differently doped regions next to each other, manufacturers create transistors. A transistor acts like an extremely small electrical switch. Billions of these switches can be placed on a processor and used to represent and process binary information.

So when a computer performs a calculation, chemistry is helping control where electrons can and cannot move.

## Slide 4 — Metals and Circuit Boards

### Put on the slide

**Circuit-board chemistry**

- Copper pathways carry current
    
- Gold resists oxidation
    
- Solder joins components
    
- Insulators prevent short circuits
    
- Chemical etching creates patterns
    

### Narration

The processor must communicate with the rest of the device, which is the job of the circuit board.

Circuit boards contain thin copper pathways called traces. Copper is useful because its metallic bonding allows electrons to move easily through it, making it an excellent electrical conductor.

Gold may be used on important connectors. Although gold is expensive, it is very resistant to oxidation and corrosion. That means it can maintain a reliable electrical connection over a long period of time.

Components are connected to the board using solder. Traditional solder often contained tin and lead, but many modern electronic products use lead-free mixtures that may contain tin, silver, and copper.

Chemistry is also involved in manufacturing the board. A copper-covered board can be coated with a light-sensitive chemical material. Light and chemical etching are then used to remove selected areas of copper and leave behind the desired circuit pattern.

The board also contains insulating materials, often made from polymers and fiberglass. These materials prevent current from flowing where it should not flow.

Therefore, a circuit board depends on a deliberate combination of conductors, insulators, alloys, polymers, and chemical manufacturing processes.

## Slide 5 — The Chemistry of Screens and Touchscreens

### Put on the slide

**How a phone detects touch**

- Transparent conductive layer
    
- Indium tin oxide
    
- Conducts electricity
    
- Allows light to pass through
    
- Touch changes the electric field
    

### Narration

The touchscreen is another example of a material that must have a very unusual combination of properties.

It needs to conduct electricity, but it also needs to be transparent. Most metals conduct electricity but block visible light.

One material commonly associated with touchscreens and flat-panel displays is indium tin oxide, or ITO. It is a solid mixture of indium oxide and tin oxide.

ITO is electrically conductive while still allowing most visible light to pass through it. A thin layer can therefore be placed over a screen without preventing us from seeing the image.

In a capacitive touchscreen, the device creates an electric field across this conductive layer. The human body also conducts electricity because it contains water and dissolved ions.

When a finger touches the screen, it changes the local electric field. Sensors detect that change and calculate the finger’s position.

Chemistry also helps produce the colors we see. Modern displays may use organic molecules, liquid crystals, or semiconductor compounds that absorb or emit particular wavelengths of light.

The phone is not simply displaying color mechanically. Its materials are interacting with electrons and photons at the atomic and molecular level.

## Slide 6 — Lithium-Ion Battery Chemistry

### Put on the slide

**Chemical energy becomes electrical energy**

- Anode
    
- Cathode
    
- Electrolyte
    
- Lithium ions move internally
    
- Electrons move through the circuit
    
- Rechargeable redox reactions
    

### Narration

A mobile phone would not be very mobile without its rechargeable battery.

A lithium-ion battery contains two electrodes: an anode and a cathode. It also contains an electrolyte that allows lithium ions to move between the electrodes.

The anode is commonly based on graphite, which is a form of carbon. The cathode is usually made from a lithium-containing metal oxide. Depending on the battery design, this material may contain cobalt, nickel, manganese, iron, or other elements.

When the battery powers a device, oxidation and reduction reactions occur.

Oxidation involves the loss of electrons, while reduction involves the gain of electrons. Together, these are called redox reactions.

During discharge, lithium atoms associated with the anode form lithium ions and release electrons. The lithium ions travel through the electrolyte inside the battery. The electrons cannot travel directly through the electrolyte, so they move through the phone’s external electrical circuit instead.

That electron movement provides the electrical energy that powers the screen, processor, speakers, and other components.

At the cathode, the electrons and lithium ions are accepted into the cathode material.

When we connect the phone to a charger, an outside source of energy forces these processes to occur in the reverse direction. Lithium ions and electrons return toward the anode, restoring the battery’s chemical potential energy.

A battery therefore does not simply “contain electricity.” It stores chemical potential energy and converts that energy into electrical energy through controlled redox reactions.

## Slide 7 — Why Batteries Can Overheat

### Put on the slide

**Battery safety is also chemistry**

- Electrolytes may be flammable
    
- Damage can cause a short circuit
    
- Heat speeds up reactions
    
- Thermal runaway
    
- Do not crush or puncture batteries
    

### Narration

The chemistry that makes lithium-ion batteries useful can also make them hazardous when they are damaged.

The anode and cathode must be kept apart by a thin separator. The separator prevents direct electrical contact while still allowing lithium ions to pass through the electrolyte.

If the battery is crushed, punctured, manufactured incorrectly, or exposed to excessive heat, the separator may fail. This can create an internal short circuit.

A short circuit releases energy very quickly and produces heat. That heat can cause additional materials inside the battery to react or break down.

Because reaction rates generally increase with temperature, the battery may enter a cycle in which heat causes reactions and those reactions produce even more heat. This process is called thermal runaway.

Some battery electrolytes contain flammable organic solvents, so a severely damaged battery can produce smoke, fire, or hot gases.

This is why swollen, punctured, or unusually hot batteries should not be used. Battery safety depends on controlling chemical reactions and keeping reactive materials physically separated.

## Slide 8 — Electronic Waste and Environmental Chemistry

### Put on the slide

**What happens after we discard a device?**

- Valuable metals can be recovered
    
- Gold, copper, nickel, and palladium
    
- Hazardous substances may be present
    
- Lead, mercury, and cadmium
    
- Reuse and responsible recycling
    

### Narration

Chemistry remains important even after a phone or computer stops working.

Electronic waste contains valuable materials such as copper, gold, nickel, indium, and palladium. Recycling can recover some of these materials and reduce the need to mine additional resources.

However, electronics can also contain hazardous substances. Depending on the device and its age, electronic waste may contain lead, mercury, cadmium, chromium, beryllium, and brominated flame retardants.

Improper recycling methods, such as burning electronic components or dissolving them in uncontrolled acids, can release harmful substances into the air, water, and soil. Workers may also be exposed to toxic metals and chemical fumes.

This demonstrates an important principle: an element’s usefulness depends on its chemical form, concentration, and location.

For example, a metal safely enclosed inside a functioning device may serve an important purpose. The same metal released into soil or drinking water could become an environmental or health hazard.

Keeping devices longer, donating functional electronics, replacing individual components when possible, and using certified recycling programs can reduce these risks.

## Slide 9 — Conclusion

### Put on the slide

**Chemistry makes digital technology possible**

- Semiconductors control electrons
    
- Metals connect components
    
- Touchscreens combine transparency and conductivity
    
- Batteries use redox reactions
    
- Recycling prevents chemical pollution
    

### Narration

In conclusion, computers and mobile phones are excellent examples of the idea that chemistry is everywhere.

Silicon’s bonding and carefully controlled impurities allow processors to manipulate electrical current. Copper, gold, solder alloys, polymers, and fiberglass create reliable circuit boards.

Transparent conductive compounds allow touchscreens to sense our fingers while still displaying an image. Rechargeable batteries use oxidation, reduction, ion movement, and electron transfer to convert chemical energy into electrical energy.

Finally, the chemistry of these devices creates environmental responsibilities. Their valuable elements can be recovered, but their hazardous materials must be managed safely.

Every time we send a message, play a game, watch a video, or use an application, we are relying on carefully selected elements and controlled chemical processes.
