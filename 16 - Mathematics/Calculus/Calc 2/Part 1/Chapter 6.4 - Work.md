# Work and Force

Work, from Physics, is the total amount of effort required to perform a task. This depends on a force, which is simply a push or pull on an object.  

In general, if an object moves along a straight line with position function $s(t)$, then the force $F$ on the object (in the same direction) is given by Newton's Second Law of Motion as the product of its mass $m$ and its acceleration $a$:

$$
F=ma =m\frac{d^2s}{dt^2}
$$

# When Force is variable

$$
W \approx \sum^{n}_{i=1}f(x^*_{i}\Delta x)
$$


As always with the limit of a Riemann sum, this approximation becomes better as $n$ becomes larger, up to infinity.

Therefore we define the work dine in moving the object from $a$ to $b$ as the limit of this quantity as $n \to \infty$.

We thus recognize this limit as a definite integral, and so

$$
\int^{b}_{a}f(x^*_{i})dx
$$

# Hooke's Law

A law from physics that states that the force required to maintain a spring stretched $x$ units beyond its natural length is proportional to $x$: So in this expression, $x$ is representative of units of length.

$$
F=f(x)=kx
$$

where $k$ is a positive constant called the **spring constant**. Hooke's Law holds provided that $x$ is not too large.

From algebraically manipulating the equation, we get:

$$
k=\frac{F}{x}
$$
