# Trig identities for Derivatives

$$
\begin{aligned}f'sinx = cosx\\
f'cosx = -sinx\\
f'tanx = sec^2x\\
f'secx = secx*tanx\\
f'cscx = -cscx*cotx\\
f'cotx = -csc^2x\\
\end{aligned}$$

# Trig Limit Identities

$$
\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{sin(x)}{x} = 1
$$

$$
\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{cos(x)-1}{x}=0
$$

# Sum identities

$$
sin(a+b) = sin(a)cos(b) + cos(a)sin(b)
$$
# Pythagorean Identities

$$
sin^2 x + cos^2 x = 1
$$
$$
1 + tan^2 x = sec^2 x
$$
$$
1 + cot^2 x = csc^2 x
$$
# Proof of the Derivative for Sinx and Cosx

The derivative of $sinx$ is $cosx$, and the derivative of $cosx$ is $-sinx$.

That's easy enough to say and memorize, but why?  Well, we can prove it fairly simply by using the limit definition of the derivative and the sum identities for $sin$.
