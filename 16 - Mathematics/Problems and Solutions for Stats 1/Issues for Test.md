# 1.) When calculating percentages, pay attention to what the question is asking for.

Ex: What percent of the males in the class consider themselves to be conservative?

Would be calculating conservative males relative to other males, not the entire class.

Pay attention to the wording.  Usually if its "all", it'll say something like "What percent of all" instead of "What percent of \<category>"

# 6.) Distribution Analysis of given scenarios

Speeding tickets in a class. We'd expect these to be unimodal and skewed to the right, because there'd be a mode at 0 or 1 and then some people with more tickets than that.

Players scores at a golf tournament.  We'd expect it to be unimodal and skewed to the right, since there would be more people with scores above the mean (since higher scores mean worse performance) than below.

Weights of female babies born in a particular hospital over the course of a year.  This should be unimodal and symmetric, since there'll be a mode near the mean and there should be roughly an equal amount of lower weight and higher weight babies on average.

The length of the average hair on the heads of student in a large class.  This should be bimodal and skewed to the right, due to the differences in gender, and because you can't have negative hair length.

What is it about running that can account for a unimodal, right-skewed histogram for a 10 year period of a runner?

It is much easier to run slower than it is to run faster than your average, and as you age you will naturally get slower or might get injured.
