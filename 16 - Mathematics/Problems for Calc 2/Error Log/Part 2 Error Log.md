# Algebra/Geometric Interpretation Error - Volume of Solids of Revolution

What happened:

For the problem 

Find the volume of the solid of revolution rotated about the axis for $y=\\sqrt{ 16-x^2}$., I didn't understand why the square root cleared when placed in the integrand.

Detection point;

was thinking of f(x) as the quantity that gets integrated.

I forgot that in the disk method, f(x) represents the **radius**, and the integrand comes from the **cross-sectional area**:

A=πr2.A=\pi r^2.A=πr2.

Since the radius is

r=16−x2,r=\sqrt{16-x^2},r=16−x2​,

the area becomes

π(16−x2)2=π(16−x2).\pi(\sqrt{16-x^2})^2 = \pi(16-x^2).π(16−x2​)2=π(16−x2).

The mistake was not identifying what geometric quantity the function represented before substituting it into the volume formula.

### Future Check

Before plugging into a volume formula, explicitly identify:

1. What does the function represent? (radius, height, width, etc.)
2. What geometric quantity is the formula asking for? (area of a disk, area of a washer, shell circumference, etc.)
3. Perform the geometric formula first, then substitute the function.
