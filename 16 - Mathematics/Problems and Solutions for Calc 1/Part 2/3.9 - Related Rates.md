# 1. Related Rate - Rectangle

The length of a rectangle is increasing at a rate of 6 cm/s and its width is increasing at a rate of 4 cm/s. When the length is 8 cm and the width is 4 cm, how fast is the area of the rectangle increasing (in cm2/s)?

We'll discuss the application of the steps here as well.

First, we identify all the variables.

Length and Width rates of increase: $6cm/s$ and $4cm/s$ respectively.
Length at the moment of time of interest and Width at the moment and time of interest: $8cm$ and $4cm$

Then, identify what the question is asking.

*How fast is the area of the rectangle increasing?*

Now, we're ready to identify what the constraint is, and the equation for it.

The $A$ of a rectangle can be represented by $A=l*w$, so to find the rate of change of the Area with respect to time, or $$\frac{dA}{dT}$$, then we need to multiply. So, $$\frac{d}{dt}(lw)$$
This is our constraint equation, now, we're ready to differentiate.
$$
\frac{d}{dt}(lw)=\frac{d}{dt}(l)*\frac{d}{dt}(w)
$$
So, then apply the product rule since we're differentiating each expression.

$$
L*\frac{dW}{dT} + W*\frac{dL}{dT}
$$
The derivative of the rate of change of each component is just the component itself, and then it's just remembering the product rule or $f'*g + g'*f$.

Now it's as simple as plugging in the values.

$$
8cm * 4cm/s + 6cm * 4cm/s
$$

Which is just
$$
32cm^{2}/s + 24cm^{2}/s=56cm^{2}/s
$$

So the $Area$ of the rectangle is increasing by $56cm^2/s$
