# (textbook) 3. Suppose you are given a formula for a function $f$.

a) How do you determine where $f$ is increasing or decreasing?

b) How do you determine where the graph of $f$ is concave upward or concave downward?

c) How do you locate inflection points?


> [!answer]- Answer
> a) Find the critical points, then find the sign of the derivative at points to the left and right of them. Where $f'(x) > 0$ the function is increasing, and where $f'(x)<0$ the function is decreasing.
> b) Concavity corresponds to the sign of the second order derivative, so find the $f''(x)$ and check possible candidates by setting it equal to zero.  Where $f''>0$, the function is concave up, and where $f''(x) < 0$ the function is concave down.
> c) Inflection points are simply where concavity changes sign, so find the intervals of concavity of either shape, and the critical points of the second derivative. Candidates are where $f''(x) = 0$ so check those points and where $f''(x) > 0$ on one side of that point and $f''(x) <0$ on the other side of that point, the function can be said to have an inflection point at that value.
