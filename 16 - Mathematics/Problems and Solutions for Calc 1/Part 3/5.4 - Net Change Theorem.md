# 9. If $w'(t)$ is the rate of growth of a child in pounds per year, what does $\int^{9}_{5}w'(t)dt$ represent?

a) The child's initial weight at birth
b) The child's weight at age 5
c) The change in the child's weight (in pounds between the ages of 5 and 9.
d) The change in the child's age (in years) between the ages of 5 and 9.
e) The child's weight at age 9.

> [!answer]- Answer
> **c) The change in the child's weight (in pounds) between the ages of 5 and 9.**
> The anti-derivative of w'(t) can be expressed simply as w(t), which would be the function of the total weight actually gained by the child. Since our bounds are 5 and 9, then the interval can be expressed as $5\leq t \leq 9$. Reading all this out, we can say that the integral of this function is representative of the total weight gained by the child between the years of 5 and 9. The answer that this matches the most is c.
