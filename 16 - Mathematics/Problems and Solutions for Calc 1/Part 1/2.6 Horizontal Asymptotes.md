# 10. Evaluating the limit using an inverse trig function

![[Pasted image 20260126144719.png]]

Answer:
	No algebra is required here.  The arctan(x) function is a compressed function with horizontal asymptotes at two constant values. Despite the presence of $e^x$, which grows without bounds towards positive $\infty$, arctan will compress it.  The question then is what value will represent an infinitely steep slope? Well, the angle for that would be 90$\degree$. In radians, this would just be $\frac{\pi}{2}$. 

# 11. Finding Horizontal and Vertical Asymptotes

![[Pasted image 20260126155605.png]]

Answer:
	![[Pasted image 20260126163904.png]]
	Factor the equation to see all discontinuities.  Wherever the factored equation's denominator has a value of x that results in undefined behavior, that will be a vertical asymptote, if it cannot be removed by cancelling.  The numerators do not matter for this. So, $-3$ and $2$ are the vertical asymptotes.  
	To find the horizontal asymptote, of which there can be a maximum of two, look at the the degrees of the two leading terms. If they are equal, take $\frac{ax}{bx}$ and divide $\frac{a}{b}$. If $a$ is $>$ $b$, then there is no asymptote. If $a$ is $<$ b, then the asymptote is $0$.
