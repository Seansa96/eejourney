The limit of the function $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $a$ is $L$.

So, let's just replace each value with what we see here.

What's $f(x)$, $a$, and $L$?

In the first limit:

* $f(x)$
* $2$
* $6$
In the second limit:

* $f(x)$
* $2$
* $8$

We also have to account for the superscript of $a$.  If it's a negative sign, that means $x$ is approaching $a$ from the left, and positive means from the right.

So, the above can be read as:

The limit of the function $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $2$ from the left is 6, and the limit of the function $f(x)$ as $x$ approaches $2$ from the right is $8$.

And then we just extrapolate the meaning again like we did in problem 1.  So, as $x$ approaches $2$ from the left, it'll get (arbitrarily) closer and closer to $6$. And as $x$ approaches $2$ from the right, it'll get closer and closer to $8$.

* a). $\lim_{x \to 2^-}f(x)$ 
* b).$\lim_{x \to 2^+}f(x)$ 
* c).$\lim_{x \to 2}f(x)$ 
* d).$f(2)$
* e).$\lim_{x \to 4}f(x)$ 
* f).$f(4)$

* For a, we want the left hand limit of $2$, which is $3$.
* For b, we want the right hand limit of $2$, which is $1$
* For c, we want to know the two sided limit of $2$. The left and right side are not equal, so the limit does not exist ($DNE$).
* For d, we just want to evaluate $f(x)$ for $2$. At $2$, $f(x)$ is $3$.
* For d, we want the two sided limit of $4$. As both limits converge upon $4$, we see that they are equal, so the limit does exist and it is $4$.

This is basically just asking us what sign would the limit be if we approach the limit from the right hand side.

In this problem, the expression we're interested in is the denominator $x-4$. Since we're coming from the right, the value will be greater than $4$, resulting in a positive number in the denominator, meaning the limit should correspondingly approach $\infty$.  If in this problem we approached from the left hand side, we would have to use a value less than $4$, which would result in a negative denominator, and thus the limit would approach $-\infty$.
