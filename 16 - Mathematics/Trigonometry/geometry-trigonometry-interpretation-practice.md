## How to work

For every problem, first draw a labeled sketch or annotate the stated geometric model. Mark right angles, parallel lines, equal lengths, angle measures, vector directions, and the particular component or region you will use. Do not rely on a sketch being to scale. Give exact answers where practical; include units and a short explanation of the geometric choice.

Questions 31–40 provide original diagrams for direct visual angle chasing. The solution key begins after Question 40. Try the full set before reading it.

## Questions

### A. Angle chasing and triangles

1. **Vertical and linear-pair angles.** Draw two lines crossing at $O$. One angle is $37^\circ$. Find the other three angles and mark the vertical pair.
   ![Crossing lines at O; one angle is 37 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-01.svg)

2. **Parallel lines.** Draw two parallel horizontal lines cut by a transversal. Mark one of the angles as $64^\circ$. Find the other seven angles, labeling which are equal and which are supplementary to the given angle.
   ![Two parallel horizontal lines cut by a transversal; one sector is 64 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-02.svg)

3. **Ramp and normal.** Sketch a ramp rising to the right at $28^\circ$ above horizontal. At a block on the ramp, draw gravity vertically downward and the inward normal perpendicular to the ramp. Find the smaller angle between these two vectors. Explain the angle relationship in your sketch.
   ![Ramp at 28 degrees, block, vertical gravity and inward normal](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-03.svg)

4. **Exterior angle.** Draw triangle $ABC$ and extend $BC$ through $C$ to $D$. If $m\angle A=42^\circ$ and $m\angle B=63^\circ$, find $m\angle ACD$ and the interior angle $m\angle C$.
   ![Triangle ABC with BC extended through C; angles A 42 degrees and B 63 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-04.svg)

5. **Isosceles triangle and bisector.** Draw an isosceles triangle with vertex angle $38^\circ$. Find each base angle. Then draw the vertex angle bisector and label the two resulting angles.
   ![Isosceles triangle with 38 degree vertex and bisector](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-05.svg)

6. **Shadow similarity.** Draw a vertical $2\text{ m}$ pole and its $3\text{ m}$ ground shadow. At the same time, a building casts a $24\text{ m}$ shadow. Use the shared sun-ray angle to construct similar triangles and find the building height.
   ![Two similar right triangles for pole and building shadows](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-06.svg)

7. **Ladder triangle.** A $13\text{ ft}$ ladder rests against a vertical wall, with its foot $5\text{ ft}$ from the wall. Draw the right triangle and find the height where the ladder touches the wall. Identify the hypotenuse before calculating.
   ![13 foot ladder, 5 foot base, vertical wall and unknown height](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-07.svg)

8. **Force components.** Draw an $80\text{ N}$ force vector at $35^\circ$ above horizontal and its horizontal and vertical component legs. Find both components, to the nearest $0.1\text{ N}$, and state why each uses sine or cosine.
   ![80 newton force 35 degrees above horizontal with component legs](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-08.svg)

9. **Torque projection.** Sketch a $0.30\text{ m}$ lever arm and a $50\text{ N}$ force making a $40^\circ$ angle with the arm. Draw the perpendicular force component. Find that component and the torque magnitude.
   ![Lever arm 0.30 m with 50 newton force at 40 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-09.svg)

10. **Recovering an angle.** Draw a right triangle whose horizontal leg is $12$ units and vertical leg is $9$ units. Find the acute angle between the horizontal leg and hypotenuse, to the nearest tenth of a degree. Label the opposite and adjacent legs for that angle.
   ![Right triangle legs 12 and 9 with angle theta at base](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-10.svg)

### B. Circle geometry

11. **Radius and tangent.** Draw a circle with center $O$ and a tangent line touching at $P$. Mark $OP=7$ and an external point $Q$ on the tangent with $OQ=25$. Find $PQ$ and mark the right angle that justifies your triangle.
   ![Circle radius 7 perpendicular to tangent; external distance 25](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-11.svg)

12. **Central and inscribed angles.** Draw a circle with center $O$ and points $A,B,C$ on the circle. Both central angle $\angle AOB$ and inscribed angle $\angle ACB$ intercept the same minor arc $AB$. If the central angle is $124^\circ$, find the inscribed angle.
   ![Central angle 124 degrees and inscribed angle intercept same arc AB](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-12.svg)

13. **Intersecting chords angle chase.** Draw chords $AC$ and $BD$ crossing at interior point $X$. The intercepted arcs $AB$ and $CD$ measure $82^\circ$ and $118^\circ$. Find $m\angle AXB$. Mark the two arcs used by this angle.
   ![Two crossing chords with arcs AB 82 degrees and CD 118 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-13.svg)

14. **Tangent-secant power.** From an exterior point $P$, draw tangent $PT$ and secant $PAB$, with $A$ the nearer intersection. If $PA=4$ and $PT=8$, find the whole secant length $PB$ and the interior portion $AB$.
   ![Tangent PT and secant PAB with PA 4 and tangent 8](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-14.svg)

15. **Sector and arc.** Draw a circle of radius $6\text{ cm}$ with a $120^\circ$ central angle. Find the area of the minor sector and the length of its minor arc. Show what fraction of the full circle the sector occupies.
   ![Circle radius 6 cm with 120 degree sector](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-15.svg)

16. **Unwrapping a cylinder.** Sketch a cylinder of radius $3\text{ cm}$ and length $10\text{ cm}$, then unwrap only its curved surface into a rectangle. Label the rectangle's dimensions and find its area.
   ![Cylinder unwrapped to rectangle, circumference 6 pi and length 10](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-16.svg)

17. **Spherical shell volume.** Draw a cross-section of a spherical shell with inner radius $4\text{ cm}$ and outer radius $7\text{ cm}$. Shade the material and find its volume.
   ![Spherical shell cross section with inner radius 4 and outer radius 7](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-17.svg)

18. **Enclosed shell charge region.** A uniformly charged spherical material fills radii from $4\text{ cm}$ to $9\text{ cm}$. Sketch a concentric Gaussian sphere of radius $6\text{ cm}$ and shade only the charged material inside it. Find the enclosed material volume.
   ![Charged spherical shell radii 4 to 9 and Gaussian sphere radius 6](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-18.svg)

### C. Geometry in vectors and physics

19. **Flux and surface normal.** Draw a flat surface of area $40\text{ cm}^2$ and its unit normal. A uniform field of magnitude $12\text{ N/C}$ makes a $60^\circ$ angle with that normal. Find the projected area perpendicular to the field and the flux magnitude.
   ![Flat area 40 square cm with normal and field at 60 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-19.svg)

20. **Two-cable equilibrium.** A $20\text{ kg}$ sign hangs motionless from two cables. In your sketch, make the left cable angle $30^\circ$ above horizontal and the right cable angle $45^\circ$ above horizontal. Find both cable tensions, using $g=9.8\text{ m/s}^2$. Draw component triangles before writing equilibrium equations.
   ![Sign weighing 20 kg supported by two cables at 30 and 45 degrees](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-20.svg)

21. **Symmetric field addition.** Two equal positive charges $+q$ sit at $(-3,0)$ and $(3,0)$. Find the direction and magnitude of the net electric field at $(0,4)$ in terms of $k$ and $q$. Draw both source-to-point right triangles and show which components cancel.
   ![Two equal charges at plus minus 3 and point P at 0,4](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-21.svg)

22. **One field vector from coordinates.** A positive point charge $q$ is at $(0,0)$ and point $P$ is at $(3,4)$. Draw the displacement triangle and the electric-field direction at $P$. Express the field's $x$ and $y$ components in terms of $k$ and $q$.
   ![Positive charge at origin and point P at (3,4) with displacement vector](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-22.svg)

23. **Choose a Gaussian surface.** Sketch and label a useful Gaussian surface for each idealized charge distribution: (a) a point charge, (b) an infinitely long line of charge, and (c) an infinite plane of charge. For each sketch, mark where the field is perpendicular to the surface and where it is parallel to the surface.
   ![Gaussian surfaces: sphere, coaxial cylinder and pillbox for symmetry](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-23.svg)

24. **Charge from a line segment.** A thin rod of total length $L$ has uniform linear charge density $\lambda$. Draw the rod, mark a segment of length $x$ from one end, and show a small element $dx$. Find the total rod charge and the charge on the marked segment.
   ![Uniformly charged rod length L with segment x and element dx](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-24.svg)

25. **Incline force components.** Draw a block on a $36^\circ$ incline, the weight vector vertically downward, and axes parallel/perpendicular to the plane. Find the magnitudes of the parallel and perpendicular components of the weight $mg$.
   ![Block on a 36 degree incline with weight and parallel normal axes](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-25.svg)

26. **Two tangents.** From an exterior point $P$, draw tangents touching a circle at $A$ and $B$. If $PA=15\text{ cm}$, find $PB$ and explain the equality from the geometry.
   ![Two tangent segments PA and PB from external point P](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-26.svg)

27. **Parallelogram area.** Draw a parallelogram with base $12\text{ cm}$, slanted side $9\text{ cm}$, and perpendicular height $7\text{ cm}$. Mark the right triangle formed by dropping the height. Find the area and explain why the slanted side is not the height.
   ![Parallelogram base 12, slanted side 9 and perpendicular height 7](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-27.svg)

28. **Circular motion directions.** Sketch a ball moving counterclockwise at the instant it is at the rightmost point of a horizontal circle. Draw its velocity and centripetal acceleration vectors. If its speed is $10\text{ m/s}$ and the radius is $5\text{ m}$, find the centripetal acceleration magnitude.
   ![Counterclockwise circular motion at rightmost point; velocity up and acceleration inward](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-28.svg)

29. **Particle deflection between plates.** A proton enters horizontally between parallel plates with speed $v_0$. The electric field is uniform and points downward. Draw the field, the initial velocity, and a short segment of the resulting trajectory. At horizontal distance $x$, derive the downward displacement $y$ in terms of $q,E,m,$ and $v_0$.
   ![Proton entering between plates with downward field and curved trajectory](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-29.svg)

30. **Mirror reflection angle chase.** Draw a horizontal mirror, a vertical normal at the point of incidence, an incoming ray that makes $32^\circ$ with the mirror, and its reflected ray on the other side of the normal. Find the smaller angle between the directions of travel of the incoming and reflected rays. Mark the equal incidence and reflection angles relative to the normal.
   ![Horizontal mirror, vertical normal and reflected ray with 32 degree mirror angle](assets/geometry-angle-diagrams/geometry-30.svg)

# Solutions

1. Opposite (vertical) angles are equal, and adjacent angles form a straight line. The four measures alternate $37^\circ,143^\circ,37^\circ,143^\circ$, since $180^\circ-37^\circ=143^\circ$.

2. The eight angles have four measures of $64^\circ$ and four measures of $116^\circ$. Corresponding and alternate interior angles match; each adjacent linear pair sums to $180^\circ$, so $180^\circ-64^\circ=116^\circ$.

3. The ramp direction is $28^\circ$ above horizontal, so its inward normal is $28^\circ$ away from vertical downward (the two angles are formed by pairs of perpendicular directions). Thus the smaller angle is $28^\circ$.

4. The exterior angle equals the sum of the two remote interior angles: $m\angle ACD=42^\circ+63^\circ=105^\circ$. The interior angle at $C$ is supplementary, so $m\angle C=180^\circ-105^\circ=75^\circ$.

5. The equal base angles share the remaining $180^\circ-38^\circ=142^\circ$, so each is $71^\circ$. The angle bisector splits the vertex angle into $19^\circ$ and $19^\circ$.

6. The triangles share the sun-ray angle and each has a right angle, so they are similar. Let $h$ be the building height. Then $h/24=2/3$, so $h=16\text{ m}$.

7. The ladder is the hypotenuse. By the Pythagorean theorem, height $h$ satisfies $h^2+5^2=13^2$, hence $h^2=144$ and $h=12\text{ ft}$.

8. The horizontal leg is adjacent to $35^\circ$: $F_x=80\cos35^\circ\approx65.5\text{ N}$. The vertical leg is opposite: $F_y=80\sin35^\circ\approx45.9\text{ N}$.

9. The perpendicular component is opposite the $40^\circ$ angle: $F_\perp=50\sin40^\circ\approx32.1\text{ N}$. Therefore $\tau=rF_\perp=(0.30)(32.1)\approx9.64\text{ N}\,\text{m}$.

10. For the base angle, opposite $=9$ and adjacent $=12$, so $\tan\theta=9/12=3/4$. Thus $\theta=\tan^{-1}(3/4)\approx36.9^\circ$.

11. A radius is perpendicular to the tangent at its point of contact, so $OPQ$ is right at $P$. Then $PQ^2+7^2=25^2$, giving $PQ=\sqrt{576}=24$.

12. An inscribed angle intercepting the same arc is half the central angle: $m\angle ACB=124^\circ/2=62^\circ$.

13. For two chords crossing inside a circle, the angle equals half the sum of the intercepted arcs for it and its vertical angle: $m\angle AXB=(82^\circ+118^\circ)/2=100^\circ$.

14. Tangent-secant power gives $PT^2=PA\cdot PB$. Thus $8^2=4\cdot PB$, so $PB=16$. The inside part is $AB=PB-PA=16-4=12$.

15. The sector is $120/360=1/3$ of the circle. Its area is $(1/3)\pi(6^2)=12\pi\text{ cm}^2$. Its arc length is $(1/3)(2\pi\cdot6)=4\pi\text{ cm}$.

16. Unwrapping makes a rectangle whose width is the cylinder circumference $2\pi r=6\pi\text{ cm}$ and whose height is $10\text{ cm}$. Area $=(6\pi)(10)=60\pi\text{ cm}^2$.

17. Subtract the inner sphere from the outer sphere: $V=\frac43\pi(7^3-4^3)=\frac43\pi(279)=372\pi\text{ cm}^3$.

18. The enclosed material is the spherical region from radius $4$ through radius $6$. Thus $V_{\rm enc}=\frac43\pi(6^3-4^3)=\frac43\pi(152)=\frac{608\pi}{3}\text{ cm}^3$.

19. Projected area is $A_\perp=A\cos60^\circ=40(1/2)=20\text{ cm}^2$. Flux magnitude is $EA\cos\theta=12(40\times10^{-4})(1/2)=0.024\text{ N}\,\text{m}^2/\text{C}$.

20. Let $T_L$ be the left tension and $T_R$ the right. Weight is $W=20(9.8)=196\text{ N}$. Horizontal balance gives $T_L\cos30^\circ=T_R\cos45^\circ$; vertical balance gives $T_L\sin30^\circ+T_R\sin45^\circ=196$. Solving yields $T_L\approx143.5\text{ N}$ and $T_R\approx175.7\text{ N}$.

21. Each charge is $5$ units from $(0,4)$, so each field magnitude is $kq/25$. The component triangles have horizontal fraction $3/5$ and vertical fraction $4/5$. Horizontal components cancel; vertical components add: $E_{\rm net}=2(kq/25)(4/5)=8kq/125$, upward.

22. The displacement has length $r=\sqrt{3^2+4^2}=5$ and unit direction $(3/5,4/5)$. Since $q>0$, the field points away from the source, toward $P$. Therefore $E_x=(kq/25)(3/5)=3kq/125$ and $E_y=(kq/25)(4/5)=4kq/125$.

23. (a) Use a sphere centered on the point charge: $\vec E$ is normal to the curved surface. (b) Use a cylinder coaxial with the line: $\vec E$ is normal on the curved side and parallel to its end caps. (c) Use a pillbox straddling the plane: $\vec E$ is normal to both flat caps and parallel to the curved side. Symmetry makes the field magnitude constant on the relevant caps or cylindrical side.

24. Uniform linear density means charge equals density times length. The whole rod has $Q=\lambda L$; the marked segment has $Q_x=\lambda x$. In differential form, $dq=\lambda\,dx$ and integrating from $0$ to $x$ gives the same result.

25. The component along the plane is opposite the $36^\circ$ angle in the force triangle, and the normal component is adjacent. Thus $W_\parallel=mg\sin36^\circ\approx0.588mg$ down the ramp and $W_\perp=mg\cos36^\circ\approx0.809mg$ into the ramp.

26. Tangent segments from the same external point have equal lengths, so $PB=PA=15\text{ cm}$.

27. Area uses base times perpendicular height: $A=12(7)=84\text{ cm}^2$. The $9\text{ cm}$ slanted edge is not perpendicular to the base; using it would overstate the height.

28. At the rightmost point, counterclockwise velocity is upward and centripetal acceleration points left, toward the center; they are perpendicular. The magnitude is $a_c=v^2/r=10^2/5=20\text{ m/s}^2$.

29. Vertical acceleration magnitude is $a=qE/m$ downward and horizontal speed remains $v_0$. Time to travel horizontal distance $x$ is $t=x/v_0$. Starting with zero vertical velocity, $y=\frac12at^2=\frac{qE}{2m}(x/v_0)^2=\frac{qEx^2}{2mv_0^2}$ downward.

30. The incoming ray makes $32^\circ$ with the mirror, so it makes $90^\circ-32^\circ=58^\circ$ with the normal. Reflection gives an equal $58^\circ$ angle on the other side. The two directions of travel are each $32^\circ$ from the horizontal on opposite sides, so their smaller separation is $32^\circ+32^\circ=64^\circ$. The $116^\circ$ angle is between the two ray arms drawn from the point, not between the travel directions.

31. The marked angles are alternate interior angles formed by parallel lines, so they are congruent. The unknown is $68^\circ$.

32. These are alternate exterior angles. Parallel lines make them congruent, so the unknown is $117^\circ$.

33. The angles occupy corresponding positions where the transversal crosses the two parallel lines. Corresponding angles are congruent, so the unknown is $52^\circ$.

34. Same-side interior angles are supplementary: $121^\circ+x=180^\circ$. Therefore $x=59^\circ$.

35. Vertical angles are congruent, so the opposite angle is $73^\circ$.

36. A linear pair sums to $180^\circ$. Thus the adjacent angle is $180^\circ-134^\circ=46^\circ$.

37. Alternate interior angles are congruent, so $x+15=75$. Therefore $x=60$. (Each marked angle is $75^\circ$.)

38. The two marked angles are same-side interior angles, so they sum to $180^\circ$. The unknown is $180^\circ-112^\circ=68^\circ$.

39. Corresponding angles are congruent: $3x-5=130$. Hence $3x=135$ and $x=45$. (The algebraic angle is $130^\circ$.)

40. The marked angles are alternate exterior angles, so $x=64^\circ$. Each obtuse angle is supplementary to an acute angle of $64^\circ$, so each of the four obtuse angles measures $180^\circ-64^\circ=116^\circ$.
