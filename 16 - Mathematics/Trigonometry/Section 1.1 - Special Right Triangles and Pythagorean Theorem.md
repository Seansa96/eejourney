
# Special Right Triangles

There are two special right triangles to recall, which both feature a **Pythagorean Triple**, which is where the side lengths are positive integers, $a,b,c$ that satisfy the equation $c^2 = a^2 + b^2$.

The first is the isosceles, which features two equal angles, and two equal sides. It is $45\degree + 45\degree + 90\degree$
This means that the hypotenuse's side of course would be twice that of either leg. 

![[Pasted image 20260219012912.png]]

The other is the scalene triangle, in which all sides are inequal, and all angles are thus inequal.  It is a $30\degree + 60\degree + 90\degree$ triangle.  The longer leg is $\sqrt3$ times the length of the shorter leg, and the hypotenuse is twice the length of that.

# Pythagorean Identity

$$
\sin^2x+\cos^2x=1
$$

$$
\sec^2-1=\tan^2
$$

$$
\sec^2-\tan^2=1
$$

$$
1+\tan^2=\sec^2
$$
