
# Histograms

These are best used to represent **quantitative variables**, like units of measurements.  You can think of these like traits that can be directly quantified, such as how long something is or how old it is.  Note that this means some variables can be both **quantitative**.  

For these kind of variables, it's difficult to naturally sort them into "piles" so to speak, so an arbitration must be made about the width of each, which can then be referred to as a **bin**.

Spaces aren't needed and in fact, will warp the data in a misleading way, as a **Histogram** is meant to represent all possible values.  Gaps in a **Histogram** are meant to represent intervals where there are no values.

# Shape of a Distribution

A distribution can be described by three main qualities: shape, center, and spread.

For shape, the way these are described are **modes**, which are basically just humps.  These are just outstanding peaks or spikes in the distribution.  Having only one peak means the mode is unimodal, while two is bimodal. Three or more are multimodal.  Modes are also sometimes referred to as the single value that appears the most.  This doesn't play well however with values that are quite precise, resulting in less common values.

A **histogram** without such peaks are referred to as uniform. The thinner sides of a distribution are referred to as the tails, and if there a side where the tail stretches further than the other, it is said to be skewed. A graph where you can fold it along a vertical line in the middle and have both sides match is said to be symmetrical.
