
# Bayesian Probability

Simply put, the probability of an event occurring, given that another event has already occurred.  

This is typically represented by 

$$
P(B|A)
$$
but the power of the theorem is the derivation, which is

$$
P(B|A)=\frac{P(A|B)*P(B)}{P(A)}
$$

and vice versa

$$
P(A|B)=\frac{P(B|A)*P(A)
}{P(B)}$$

## Example 1

Given two events, $A$ and $B$, find 

$P(B|A)$

and

$P(A|B)$

where $$
A={1,2,3,4,5}
$$
$$
B={4,5,6,7,8,9}
$$

## Example 2

A particular study showed that 12% of men will likely develop prostate cancer at some point in their lives. A man with prostate cancer has a 95% chance of a positive test result from a medical screening exam. A man without prostate cancer has a 6% chance of getting a false positive test result. What is the probability that a man has cancer given that he has a positive test result?

**Guiding Note:** For every probability given, attempt to label it. What is $P(A)$? What is $P(B)$? And so on.  You don't need to label it $A$ or $B$ though. These are just dummy variables.
