You can use a strategy called synthetic division to long-divide polynomials, typically by a linear binomial of the form $x-c$, where $c$ is a real number.

Say we have

$$
\frac{x^3-2x^2-5x+6}{x-3}
$$

We draw the typically long division structure, then use the zero for the denominator, that being $x=3$, as the divisor, and the dividend is the coefficients of the polynomial kept in the same order. You then bring down the first coefficient, multiply it by the divisor, then add that product to the next coefficient, repeating the process until you have nothing left to add to. If your remainder is zero, then it is a factor; if it is not, then it is not a factor.
