# Definition of Arithmetic Sequences

A sequence $a_{1},a_{2},\dots a_{n}\dots$ is an arithmetic sequence if there is a real number $d$ such that for every possible integer $k$,

$$
a_{k+1}=a_{k}+d
$$

The number $d$, equal to $a_{k+1}-a_{k}$ is called the common difference of the sequence.

Note that the common difference $d$ is the difference of any two successive terms of an arithmetic sequence.

# Theorem: Formula for $S_n$

If $a_1,a_2,a_3,...,a_n,...$ is an arithmetic sequence with common difference $d$, then the nth partial sum $s_n$, that is, the sum of the first $n$ terms is given by eiher:

$$
S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}[2a_{1}+(n-1)d]
$$
or

$$
S_{n}=\frac{n}{2}(a_{1}+a_{2})
$$
