Logarithms are a way to represent the function

$(N)^{x} = V$

The inverse function:

$log_{N}(V) = x$

They are the inverse of [[16 - Mathematics/exponents|exponentiation]], and answer the questions raised by the above functions.

Where N is the given base for that particular number system, and x is the power that number must be raised to in order to resolve for the intended decimal value.

The natural log [[Euler's Number|e]] is one of the best ways to demonstrate exponential growth, and its inverse, $ln(e)$, solves for 1, the [[multiplicative identity]]. 

Logarithms historically had applications as extended multiplication tables of sorts, allowing academics and scholar of the various scientific disciplines the ability to perform normally long and unwieldy calculations like the multiplication or division of large integers with massively increased speed and ease.

This is because by representing a value in its logarithmic form, the operation to achieve the solution is changed to simple addition or subtraction. By processing the combined logarithmic values, one can then simply look up the mapped value, given that it has been calculated and written down.

While this historical function has largely been replaced by more robust automated tooling and electronics, the importance of logarithms remain in encoding information, optimizing large calculations in a compute space, simplifying floating point calculations, and offering visualization aid in large scale exponential growth or decay within a large range such as with earthquakes, pH, decibels, etc.
