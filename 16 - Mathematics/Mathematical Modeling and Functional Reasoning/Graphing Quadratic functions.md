A quadratic function is just a function whose highest exponent is $2$.

All quadratics come in the form $ax^2 + bx + c$. The first thing to do when graphing a quadratic is to determine the axis of symmetry, or the "balancing point" of the parabola.

You can acquire the axis of symmetry with the formula $$
\frac{-b}{2a}
$$
# Example 1

$$
x^2 -8x+15
$$

In this case, $b=-8$ and $a=1$, so $$
\frac{-b}{2a} = \frac{8}{2}
$$
Which is just $4$. So at $x=4$, we can plot a dotted vertical line that the parabola halves can be congruent to.

To obtain the vertex, we need both the $x$ and $y$ coordinate, and to obtain the $y$ value, of course, we just plug $x$ into the quadratic.

After that, we want to pick points that are pretty close to the vertex since quadratics blow up quickly.  And, since they're congruent, once we have one side we can take a shortcut by just mirroring the approximation on the other side. Or we can actually do the math. Either or.
