The cross section of a 2D or 3D geometric form is that of the shape created by intersecting the form either parallel or perpendicular or some other arbitrary angle to its base.

The following basic example can be seen here, with a square prism.

# Example 1 - Square Prism

![[Pasted image 20260512194938.png]]

This is a cross-section formed by a plane slicing through parallel to the base.

If we perform a cross-sectional slice perpendicular to the base, we get a rectangle.

![[Pasted image 20260512195244.png]]


In this sense, a cross-sectional area can be thought of the shape formed after a slice parallel, perpendicular, or otherwise, has taken off that amount of area from the original shape.  We see that it is in many cases a projection of the "top" or "side" form.
