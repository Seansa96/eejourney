# What's a circle?

Mathematically, a circle defined as the set of all points which are a fixed distance from a specific point.  The point in question is referred to as the **center**.

![[Pasted image 20261027010722.png]]

Looking here, we see that $O$ is the center. That **fixed distance** is then the **radius**. A **chord** (AC) of a circle is a segment whose endpoints are both on the circle. So $AB$ would also qualify. 

A **diameter** is just a segment which passes through the **center**. Thus, $AC$ is a chord but not a diameter. $AB$ is both a chord and a diameter. A **tangent** is a line which touches the circle in only one place (which is only possible at one of the points about the endpoint of a radius not at the center), and a **secant** is a line which passes *through* the circle, intersecting it in two places, ($m$).

Circles are often referred to by their centers, and so we can call the circle in the figure, circle $O$. 

Circles that have the same center are called **concentric**.

A part of the curve of a circle is called an **arc**, and is denoted $\widehat{AC}$, where $A$ and $C$ are the endpoints.

This notation isn't exactly clear, and can refer to two different arcs--the long way around the circle or the short way, so often three points are used to designate the arc: $\widehat{ABC}$. 

If only two points are used to designate the ac, then it is assumed that we mean the shorter one, called the **minor** arc.  

The larger arc of course would be the **major** arc. The area inside a circle that is cut off off by two radii is called a **sector**, and a circular segment is the area between a chord and the arc it intercepts.

A diameter is then obviously twice the radius, being in effect just two radii laid end-to-end.  The circumference of a circle is simply the arc length of a closed curve that goes around or "trace" the circle exactly once.

For ALL circles, the ratio of this circumference to the diameter is exactly $\pi$. Thus, we see that

$$
\pi= \frac{C}{d}; C=\pi d;C=\pi(2r);C=2\pi r
$$
The area inside a circle of radius $r$ is $\pi r^2$.
