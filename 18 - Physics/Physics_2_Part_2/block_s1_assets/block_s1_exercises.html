Series Equivalent Resistance from KVL [2 points]: Consider $N$ resistors $R_1, R_2, \dots, R_N$ connected in series across terminals $a$ and $b$ with terminal potential difference $V_{ab} = V_a - V_b$. Starting from steady-state charge conservation ($\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J} = 0$) and Kirchhoff's loop rule ($\oint \mathbf{E}\cdot d\mathbf{l} = 0$), derive the series equivalent resistance formula $R_{\text{eq}} = \sum_{k=1}^{N} R_k$.
  2. Parallel Equivalent Resistance from KCL [2 points]: Consider $N$ resistors $R_1, R_2, \dots, R_N$ connected in parallel between common nodes $a$ and $b$. Starting from Kirchhoff's junction rule ($\sum I_{\text{in}} = \sum I_{\text{out}}$) and the path-independence of electrostatic potential, derive the reciprocal parallel formula $\frac{1}{R_{\text{eq}}} = \sum_{k=1}^{N} \frac{1}{R_k}$ and prove algebraically why $R_{\text{eq}} < \min_{1\le k\le N}(R_k)$ whenever all $R_k > 0$.
  3. Continuous Tapered Conductor — "Problem with Calculus" [6 points]: A solid conductor of length $L$ and uniform resistivity $\rho$ has the shape of a truncated right circular cone (frustum) whose circular cross-section tapers linearly from radius $a$ at $x = 0$ to radius $b$ at $x = L$, as shown below. Assume steady current $I$ flows axially along $+x$ with uniform current density over each circular cross-section.
    1. Write the radius $r(x)$ and cross-sectional area $A(x) = \pi [r(x)]^2$ at position $x \in [0, L]$, and express the differential resistance $dR$ of the shaded disk slice of thickness $dx$. [2 pts]
    2. Integrate $R = \int_0^L dR$ using a clear $u$-substitution to prove that the exact resistance between the two flat circular ends is $R = \dfrac{\rho L}{\pi a b}$. [3 pts]
    3. Show that your expression reduces to the elementary cylinder resistance formula in the limit $b \to a$, and write the axial electric field magnitude $E(x)$ inside the conductor as a function of $x$. [1 pt]

02 · Mixed Series–Parallel DC Ladder & Short-Circuit Perturbation Ch. 26 • 10 Points • FR + SPEC V2

An ideal DC voltage source $V_s = 36.0\text{ V}$ supplies a series resistor $R_1 = 6.00\ \Omega$ connected at junction $B$ to two parallel branches returned to ground node $G$ ($V_G = 0\text{ V}$): the middle branch contains $R_2 = 18.0\ \Omega$ alone, and the right branch contains $R_3 = 4.00\ \Omega$ in series with $R_4 = 5.00\ \Omega$ joined at intermediate node $D$.

  1. Equivalent Resistance Reduction [2 points]: Find the equivalent resistance $R_{BG}$ of the parallel block between node $B$ and ground $G$, and find the total equivalent resistance $R_{\text{eq}}$ connected across the $36.0\text{ V}$ source.
  2. Node Potentials & Branch Currents [3 points]: Calculate the source current $I_{\text{src}}$, the node potential $V_B$, the parallel branch currents $I_2$ and $I_{34}$, and the intermediate node potential $V_D$. Show that $I_2$ and $I_{34}$ satisfy both KCL at node $B$ and the two-branch current-divider formula.
  3. Joule Heating & Power Balance [2 points]: Calculate the electrical power dissipated in each of the four resistors ($P_{R_1}, P_{R_2}, P_{R_3}, P_{R_4}$) and verify that $\sum_{k=1}^4 P_{R_k}$ equals the total power supplied by the $36.0\text{ V}$ source.
  4. Short-Circuit Perturbation [3 points]: Suppose a conducting jumper wire accidentally shorts node $B$ directly to node $D$ (bypassing $R_3$, so $R_3 \to 0$). Calculate the new total equivalent resistance $R_{\text{eq}}'$, the new source current $I_{\text{src}}'$, and the new power $P_{R_4}'$ dissipated in $R_4$. By what numerical ratio $P_{R_4}'/P_{R_4}$ does the heating in $R_4$ increase?

  1. KCL & KVL Equations [3 points]:
