Key equations:

$$
\vec E=\frac{\vec F}{q_0},\qquad \vec F=q\vec E

Key equations:

$$
\vec p=q\vec d
$$

$$
\vec\tau=\vec p\times\vec E
$$

For the first equation, $q$ is the positive charge magnitude and $\vec d$ points from negative to positive.

The field exerts equal and opposite forces on the charges, producing a torque that aligns the dipole moment with the field.

# A framework for your teach-back videos

I'd suggest structuring each video around the same sequence, so that you develop a repeatable way of explaining physics.

1. Introduce the physical question

What phenomenon are we trying to explain, and why does it matter?

2. Build physical intuition

Use an example, a diagram, or a thought experiment before introducing equations.

3. Develop the mathematical model

Define quantities, introduce equations, explain assumptions, and derive relationships where appropriate.

4. Solve a representative problem

Explain how you recognize the problem type, select a method, and check the result.

5. Test the model's limits

Ask what changes if you reverse the charge, double the distance, change the geometry, or remove an assumption.

## The distinction I'd emphasize most

There is a difference between being able to solve a physics problem and being able to explain the physics that makes the solution valid.

For example, consider the familiar equation:

$$
E=k\frac{|Q|}{r^2}
$$

At a basic level, you should know how to substitute values to calculate electric field strength.

At a deeper level, you should be able to explain:

- Why field strength decreases with distance.
    
- Why the magnitude depends on the source charge but not the test charge.
    
- Why field contributions can cancel even when their individual magnitudes are nonzero.
    
- Why the equation works for point charges and outside spherically symmetric charge distributions, but cannot automatically be applied to an arbitrary extended object.
    
- How the result relates to the force experienced by another charge placed in the field.
    

That deeper level is what I would target in your videos.

One final recommendation: don't feel obligated to reproduce the textbook's order in your actual videos. The chapter's sections are a good organizational structure for reviewing, but the most intuitive explanation might begin with Coulomb's law, use it to motivate electric fields, then return to how charges behave inside matter.

If you can transition between the physical interpretation, mathematical representation, and geometric visualization of an idea without losing the thread, you're developing the understanding needed not just to teach introductory electromagnetism, but also to approach more advanced EE topics confidently.
