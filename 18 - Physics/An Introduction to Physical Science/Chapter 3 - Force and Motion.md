
# 3.1 - Force and Net Force

**Force** can be defined as a vector quantity capable of producing motion or a change in motion, that is, a change in velocity or an acceleration.

The word capable here expands what can be determined to be a force as more than just something that causes another thing to exhibit a change in velocity or acceleration. If that were true, then any force could cause a change, which is known to be untrue. If you push an object with great mass, for example, you will be unlikely to cause it to accelerate in the direction of your push. This is because the force of the object pushing back against you cancels this out, due to its inertia, or its mass.

So it can be said then that motion occurs only when the net forces acting on an object are unbalanced.

# Newton's First Law of Motion

An object at rest or in uniform motion in a straight motion will remain as such unless acted on by an external, unbalanced force.

Newton discovered and formalized this thought after Aristotle and Galileo theorized the natural state of motion of an object.

Aristotle was of the opinion that the natural state of an object is to be at rest, while Galileo through an experiment involving rolling a ball down an inclined plane, discovered that the smoother he made the plane, the longer the ball rolled.

Thus, Galileo was able to add further clarification to Aristotle's hypothesis, and determined that an object could have the natural state to be in uniform motion as well as at rest.
