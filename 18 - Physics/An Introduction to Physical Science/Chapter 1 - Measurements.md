# 1.1 - The Physical Sciences

The Sciences are split up into the Natural Sciences and Social Sciences. The Natural Sciences are further subdivided into the Physical Sciences and the Biological Sciences.

The Physical Sciences are divided into 5: **Physics**, **Chemistry**, **Astronomy**, **Meteorology**, and **Geology**.

**Astronomy, Meteorology, and Geology** are further categorized as **Earth and Space Sciences**.

**Physics** deals with the basic principles and concepts of matter and energy.

**Chemistry** deals with the composition, structure, and reactions of matter.

**Astronomy** is the study of the universe which is the totality of all matter, energy, space, and time.

**Meteorology** is the study of the atmosphere, from the surface of the Earth to where it ends in outer space.

**Geology** is the science of the planet Earth: its composition, structure, processes, and history.

## The Scientific Method

The scientific method, which is the basis for interrogation of those concerns of the above, is thus:

1. Observations and measurements (quantitative data).
2. Hypothesis. A possible explanation or educated guess.
3. Experiments. Testing of the hypothesis under controlled conditions.
4. Theory. If a hypothesis passes enough experimental tests and generates new predictions, then it can because a theory, or a well-tested explanation.
5. Law. If a theory withstands the test of many well-designed, valid experiments, and there is great regularity in the results, then that theory may be accepted by scientists as a law.

# 1.4 - Standard Units and Systems of Units

The most fundamental physical quantities are that of length, mass, and time.  These are the most basic quantities we can imagine.

The topic of **mechanics**, that being the study of motion and force, only requires these physical quantities.

So in this, we typically employ the use of a reference, or a standard unit. That is, a fixed and reproducible value for the purpose of taking accurate measurements.

A group of standard units and their combinations is called a system of units.  

The fundamental quantity **length** is defined as the measurement of space in any direction. Space has three dimensions, each of which can be measured in terms of length.  

These three dimensions are commonly represented by a three-dimensional Cartesian coordinate system in honor of the French mathematician [[Rene Descartes]], who developed it.

The **standard unit of length** is the meter (m), from the Greek *metron*, 'to measure'. 1 meter is slightly longer than one yard. This was derived by measuring part of the meridian between Dunkirk, France, and Barcelona to determine the meter length, and then one ten-millionth of that distance was determined to be a meter. The **current definition** is instead defined as the distance light travels in 1/299,792,458th of a second.

Mass is the amount of matter an object contains, and also a measure of its inertia.  The more massive an object, the more matter it contains, and the greater force required to change its velocity or accelerate it.

The standard unit of measurement of mass is the **kilogram**. Remember that this is different from **weight**, which is the force of gravitational force exerted on an entity by local gravitational fields, typically caused by celestial bodies.

**Time** has many definitions and interpretations philosophically and amongst the physical sciences. The simplest definition is that Time is the continuous, forward flow of events.

## 1.5 - Volume


The unit of fluid volume or capacity is the Liter, in which $1L$ has a volume of $10cm \times 10cm \times 10cm$. The kilogram was originally defined as the mass of a cube of water with side lengths of $10cm$, so $1000cm$ is equal to $1000g$, and $1000mL$, meaning that $1g$=$1mL$, and $1cm^3$ is equal to both of those.

$1cm^3=1mL=1g$


## 1.6 - Derived Units and Conversion Factors

The density of a substance describes the compactness of its matter or mass. It can thus be thought of as the mass per unit volume, represented by the equation

$$
p=\frac{m}{v}
$$
A hydrometer is used to measure the density of a liquid. The denser the liquid, the higher the hydrometer floats, and its density can be read from the stem.
