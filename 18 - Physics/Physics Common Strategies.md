
# Eliminating Time Reveals Geometry

Used when

* Trajectory shape matters
* Orbits matter
* Field lines matter
* Path structure matters
This will reveal geometric curves, symmetry, and spatial relationships.

# Replacing Position with Energy

Motion can be difficult in terms of forces and acceleration, but easy in terms of energy.

So, instead of say

$$
F=ma
$$
We use

K+U=constant

This essentially switches local force behavior for global state constraints

Very good for mechanics


# Replacing Time with Angle

Common in rotational systems.

Instead of:

linear displacement $x$ and linear velocity $v$

We use:

Angular Displacement $\theta$ and Angular velocity $\omega$ 

This forces rotational symmetry

# Replacing Cartesian Coordinates with Polar Coordinates

Useful for circles, rotations, radial symmetry, orbital motion, because $r$ can be thought of as constant instead, simplifying things.

# Replacing Velocity with Momentum

Instead of tracking $v$ which may not be important, you just track

$$
p=mv
$$
because collisions become dramatically simpler

# Replacing Force with Potential

Instead of thinking of a Force acting at every point, you just put it in terms of the energy landscape or system.

This turns differential-force thinking into geometric-energy thinking.

# Moving Into Another Frame

Sometimes problems are difficult in one frame but very easy in another.

Things like rotating systems, moving observers, relativity, and center-of-mass motion, these can undergo large changes in complexity simply by switching frames.
