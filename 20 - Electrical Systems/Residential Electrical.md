
# Circuit Breakers

Circuit breakers are used on the main service panel to regulate current to various areas of the residence.  They serve as protection for the circuit and will trip if it detects excess current, which usually results in overheating. The range of amperage supplied varies from 10-40 amps, although you may see 20A being the most common.

Short circuits cause instantaneous trips, while overloads (which is when the ampacity of a circuit is reached) result in shorter trips due to a more gradual circumstance, but will still cause a trip in a functioning circuit breaker.


# Wires

## Colors

*  **Black/Red** - Wire supplying the current to the load
*  **White** - Wire completing the circuit back to the power source
*  **Green or bare copper** - Ground wire

## Cabling

Separated by gauges. Lower gauge number is a thicker cable. Reference to the original process of wire drawing, where the gauge represented the number of pulls through a cable die. The more pulls, the thinner the material, so the less pulls, the thicker. Cables are bundled wires.  The rating of the cabling can be seen usually by the labeling on the sheath.  A 10-2 gauge for example will be 10 gauge with 2 conductor wires. One being black, one being white, and then a ground.

14 gauge is white, 12 is yellow, 10 is orange, typically.

Thicker wires can take more current without heating up.
